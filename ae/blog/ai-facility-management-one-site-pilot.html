AI in facility management: where to start with a one-site pilot

The first useful question is not which AI tool to buy. It is which building workflow you can describe from the incoming information to a responsible person's action. A one-site pilot gives that question a practical boundary: one location, one process and an agreed way to compare what happens before and after the change.

Follow one request through the current process

Begin with a request your facilities team already handles. Establish where it arrives, what information staff consult and how a responsible team is selected. Then follow the request to the record of its outcome. This is an audit task, not an assumption that every building has the same operational problem. The point is to identify the actual handover that the pilot will examine.

Write the process in the language your team uses. If people disagree about who acts after a request is classified, resolve that question before discussing automation. A recommendation needs an owner. Otherwise, the pilot may produce an additional summary without changing the workflow it was meant to test. This is why the AI facility management UAE service starts with request intake, routing and a recorded action.

Choose a boundary the building team can recognise

Selecting one site is only the first boundary. The pilot also needs an agreed request category and a clear description of the output. Decide what enters the experiment and what remains in the existing process. Staff should know whether they are looking at a recommendation, a request for clarification or a handover that needs their attention.

This does not require pretending that the rest of the operation is simple. It means choosing a part that can be examined without changing several unrelated workflows at once. If a question falls outside that boundary, record it for review. Expanding the scope during implementation can make the final comparison difficult to interpret, because the original experiment and the delivered workflow no longer describe the same thing.

Check what information is actually available

ST8 AI's published service areas include document processing, task routing and reporting. Those capabilities still need a concrete source in your operation. Identify the relevant reports, system exports or spreadsheets and the person responsible for access. A system name alone does not establish that its information can be used in the proposed pilot.

The international service model places an AI layer over existing ERP, CRM, document systems and tables. During the audit, check the available exports or APIs and agree the connection scope. Keep the information used for a recommendation traceable to its source. This makes it possible to review a questionable output with the staff who understand the original request, rather than debate a summary without context.

Define the action after the analysis

A simple proposed flow is information, analysis, exception or recommendation, then responsible action. For the pilot, describe each handover in that chain. Where will the team see the output? What needs review? Where will the decision or completion be recorded? These questions turn an AI feature into a workflow that can be evaluated.

Treat unclear cases as part of the experiment. Agree how staff will take them over and what they should record afterwards. The aim is not to count only clean examples. Exceptions help explain the limits of the selected process and the preparation needed before a wider rollout. They also keep the final discussion connected to the actions people actually took.

Agree the baseline before changing the workflow

Select indicators that describe the chosen process and establish their current values from the available records. Agree the comparison period and any operating conditions that need to be considered. The existing ST8 AI pilot approach fixes KPI definitions and the measurement method before launch, rather than choosing a convenient interpretation after the results arrive.

Possible review topics include how requests are handed over, how exceptions are handled and whether the responsible action is recorded. These are proposed measurement areas, not reported improvements for your building. The contract should say which indicators will actually be used. A broad annual saving from a calculator cannot replace the evidence needed for a specific FM workflow.

Use the operation period to learn about the scope

The existing UAE page describes launch in about 7–10 days and roughly 3–4 weeks of live operation, with scope and data access agreed first. Use that period to review the workflow with the responsible team. Keep the discussion focused on the selected process, the information used and the outcomes that can be checked against the baseline.

At the final review, separate what the records show from what still needs investigation. The decision might be to refine the workflow before considering another location. There is no need to treat a working connection as proof that a wider rollout is ready. The purpose of a pilot is to support a decision with the evidence from its agreed boundary.
