The problem: several restaurant tasks compete for the same attention

Bookings, guest questions, purchasing and kitchen planning are different workflows. Combining them into a single brief makes it difficult to see what has changed. We start by identifying the process you want to improve and the person who will use the system's output.

Restaurant AI automation can begin with guest communication or with an operational process behind the kitchen. The choice depends on available data and a clear next action. A list of possible modules is useful for discussion, but the pilot needs a boundary that staff can recognise during a normal shift.

What can enter the first pilot

Guest enquiries and bookings

A digital administrator can be scoped around bookings and messenger enquiries. For a UAE pilot, we agree the channel, supported questions and handover to staff. A question outside the approved information becomes a handover case, rather than an opportunity to invent an answer.

Voice-host scope

An AI voice host for a restaurant needs an agreed language, channel and staff escalation path. A voice assistant is a custom-development scope that needs its own connection and conversation rules. Availability for a particular restaurant depends on the audit; the pilot brief must define the actual conversations and connection requirements before implementation.

Kitchen planning and purchasing

Dark kitchen automation can focus on demand information, production planning, stock or purchasing recommendations. The kitchen-planning case linked below shows an example of this type of process. For your pilot, we select one output and agree who reviews it before it affects the operation. We do not treat a general calculator as evidence of a restaurant's actual result.

How we run the pilot on one location

Describe the process

Choose a restaurant or kitchen, identify the source information and record the current workflow. For enquiries, follow a conversation to its staff handover. For planning, follow the information to the person making the operational decision.

Agree the connection

ST8 AI is an AI layer over the current POS, ERP, CRM or spreadsheets. We check the available exports or APIs and set the connection scope during the audit. The pilot does not require a promise that every tool will be replaced or every task automated.

Measure the agreed change

The proposed timetable is about 7–10 days to launch and roughly 3–4 weeks of operation, with scope and data access agreed first. KPIs and the comparison method are written into the contract. At review, examine actual records and exceptions alongside the baseline, then decide whether the same workflow should be expanded.

What to send before the first call

Describe one task, your current system and the person responsible for the outcome. If the priority is bookings, say which channel receives them. If it is kitchen planning, explain which information is already available. This gives the audit a concrete starting point and makes it possible to agree a limited pilot without inventing a saving or quoting an unrelated project as your forecast.

Frequently asked questions

Can we start with one restaurant?

Yes. Select one location and one workflow, then agree the data and the person responsible for its output.

Can the scope include a voice host?

Voice assistants are within the published custom-development scope. Language, channel and connection requirements must be confirmed in the audit.

Does dark kitchen automation require new systems?

The pilot is proposed above existing systems and spreadsheets. We check the available connection before agreeing the scope.

Is the calculator result a guarantee?

No. A model estimate is separate from measured restaurant results. Pilot KPIs and the comparison method are agreed beforehand.

When can a pilot be reviewed?

The existing model uses about 7–10 days for launch and roughly 3–4 weeks of operation, subject to the agreed scope.
