Automating Your Business with AI: Where to Start

A step-by-step guide for those considering AI implementation in their business but unsure which direction to start with.

When a business owner decides to look into AI-based automation, they usually run into the same problem: too many promises and too little specifics. "Artificial intelligence will increase profit" is a phrase that says nothing about what will actually change on Monday morning. Below is a practical plan for where to genuinely start.

Step 1. Choose a Direction, Not a Technology

Don't start with the question "which neural network should we implement" — start with "which process costs us the most money or time." At ST8 AI, solutions are grouped by business task, not by technology:

  • HoReCa — digital host, predictive purchasing, inventory management, kitchen computer vision (more in the article "Automating a Restaurant with Neural Networks").
  • Manufacturing — computer vision for quality control, predictive equipment maintenance (more in the article "Implementing AI in Manufacturing").
  • Retail & e-commerce — demand forecasting, automated purchasing, personalized recommendations.
  • Logistics — route optimization, order verification, warehouse load forecasting.
  • Offices & property management — document recognition, request routing, automated analytics.

See the full list in the "Solutions" section on the homepage.

Step 2. Assess Whether You Have Data

A common worry is "we don't have data to train the model." In practice, what's already in your accounting system is usually enough to get started: POS receipts, CRM, inventory records. The model keeps learning as data accumulates during the pilot itself — it doesn't require a perfect dataset upfront.

Step 3. Start with a Pilot at One Location

The right rollout order is: first a pilot at one restaurant, one line, or one warehouse, and only then scaling to the whole network. This reduces risk — if something doesn't work as expected, you lose the time and budget of one pilot, not the entire network at once.

Step 4. Fix KPIs and Exit Terms in the Contract

Automation is an investment, and like any investment, it needs measurable success criteria fixed in advance — not "let's see how it goes." Working with ST8 AI, this looks like:

  • KPI guarantee — target metrics are fixed in the contract before work begins.
  • Pilot at 1 location — full rollout only after a confirmed result.
  • Exit without penalty — 30 days to exit the project with no penalties.
  • City exclusivity — ST8 AI doesn't work with your direct competitors in the same city.

Step 5. Estimate the Approximate Cost

ST8 AI's indicative pricing scales with business size: from ₽65,000 for 1-3 locations, from ₽165,000 for 4-10 locations, from ₽385,000 for a network of 10+ locations — with a more precise ROI calculation based on your numbers in the calculator on the homepage. Exact cost and timelines for your case can be discussed directly, based on scale and the chosen direction.

Step 6. Look at Real Cases, Not Just Promises

Before making a decision, it helps to see what implementation actually looks like in practice — not in a presentation, but in a working project. For example:

  • ST8 Dark Intelligence — AI production planning for the dark kitchen chain Azbuka Vkusa.
  • ST8 BUILD — AI Master — automated cost estimates and schedule control for developer Level Group.
  • CHEFF AI — an AI process engineer for the dark kitchen Unik Food: nutrition facts, process specs, HACCP.

See the full list on the "Cases" page.

Bottom Line

Automating a business with AI works best not as a one-off "implement a neural network" project, but as a sequential process: identify the most expensive bottleneck → launch a pilot at one location with fixed KPIs → scale across the whole business after the result is confirmed. That's exactly how all 47 of ST8 AI's projects were structured — 0 of them failed to reach a result.
