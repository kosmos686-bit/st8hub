What the solution includes

  • Route optimisation — mileage reduced by 25%, accounting for traffic and time windows
  • Computer vision — order completeness check at dispatch, protection from errors
  • Load forecast — warehouse capacity planning for 14 days
  • Real-time tracking — GPS + AI arrival-time forecast
  • Eco-driving — driving style analysis, lower fuel consumption

How it works

Integration with WMS/TMS (timing is indicative, after scope and access are confirmed). The AI builds routes using 50+ parameters. The system adapts to changes in real time — an accident on the MKAD = route rebuilt in 30 seconds.

How each module works

Route optimisation. The AI builds routes accounting for addresses, delivery time windows, vehicle capacity and volume, traffic, recipients' working hours and entry restrictions. The dispatcher gets a ready plan for the day and can adjust it by hand, and with new orders or disruptions the route is rebuilt without recalculating the whole plan from scratch. Less mileage and empty runs means lower fuel consumption and less driver overtime.

Arrival time forecast. From GPS data and trip history the system calculates the real arrival time at each customer and warns about delays in advance — the dispatcher and the customer learn about the problem before it happens.

Order completeness control with computer vision. Cameras at the picking and dispatch area check the assembled order against the specification: number of packages, labelling, product match. The error is caught before dispatch, not after a customer complaint.

Warehouse load forecast. The model forecasts the volume of incoming deliveries and dispatches by day and shift so that staff, docks and equipment can be planned in advance and people are not left idle on quiet days.

Rollout stages

  • Process audit — we look at how trips are planned today, how orders are picked and where errors and overtime arise.
  • Integration with WMS/TMS (timeline set once scope and access are confirmed) — we connect to the systems in use without stopping the warehouse or the transport.
  • Model training — on trip history, GPS tracks, orders and warehouse operations.
  • Pilot at one warehouse or fleet — we compare mileage, fuel consumption, delivery time and number of mis-sorts with the baseline.
  • Scaling — connecting the remaining warehouses, branches and contractors.

What data is needed

Order and trip history, customer addresses with time windows, vehicle specifications, GPS tracks (if vehicles have trackers) and warehouse system data. If part of the information is currently kept in spreadsheets, we structure it at the integration stage.

Typical mistakes when introducing AI in logistics

  • Not building in real constraints. A route that ignores unloading time, the customer's working hours or a truck entry ban looks optimal only on the map.
  • Taking the decision away from the dispatcher. The best results come when the AI proposes a plan, an experienced dispatcher can adjust it, and the system learns from those edits.
  • Installing cameras without changing the process. Computer vision has an effect when the picker has a clear action on a detected error — repick, call a senior, block dispatch.
  • Not measuring the starting point. Without measuring mileage, fuel and mis-sorts before the start, savings cannot be calculated honestly.

Who it suits

Transport and logistics companies, 3PL operators, distributors with their own fleet, delivery services and companies with their own warehouses — wherever dozens of trips are planned or hundreds of orders are picked every day.

What it looks like on an ordinary day

The evening before, the system collects tomorrow's orders and builds routes accounting for vehicles, drivers, customer time windows and entry restrictions. In the morning the dispatcher checks the plan, makes edits for situations only they know, and sends routes to the drivers. During the day urgent orders arrive — the route is rebuilt for vehicles that can still pick them up. If a driver is stuck in traffic, the system recalculates the arrival time and warns in advance the customers whose delivery shifts. In the warehouse cameras check the completeness of each order before dispatch and the storekeeper immediately sees where the error is. In the evening the head of logistics receives a summary of mileage, delays and picking errors, and the model accounts for the day's actual data when planning the next.

Working with contractors and hired transport

If part of the transport is carried out by third-party carriers, their vehicles can be planned on a par with your own fleet: the system suggests which trips are more profitable to give to a contractor and which to cover with your own transport.

What changes for the team

  • The dispatcher starts the day with a ready route plan instead of hours of manually spreading orders across vehicles, and deals with exceptions.
  • The driver gets a clear route with the order of stops and time windows, drives empty less, and runs into “the customer has already closed” less often.
  • The storekeeper and picker learn about a picking error at the dispatch area immediately, not from a customer complaint.
  • The head of logistics sees mileage, fuel consumption, compliance with delivery windows and picking errors in one report across warehouses and vehicles.

Integrations

We connect to the WMS and TMS in use, the 1C-based accounting system and vehicle GPS monitoring systems. Ready routes are passed to dispatchers and drivers in the tools they are used to, and execution data returns for analysis and retraining of the model.

How we measure the effect

Before the pilot we fix mileage and fuel consumption on the chosen fleet, the share of deliveries outside the time window, the number of mis-sorts and claims, and the time spent planning trips. After the pilot we compare the same metrics over a comparable period at a comparable order volume.

When rollout does not pay off

If a company has a few vehicles, regular customers and the same routes every day, optimisation will give a small gain — an experienced dispatcher already knows the best order of stops. Computer vision in the warehouse is excessive if orders are few and picking errors rare. The greatest effect comes where there are many changing orders, tight time windows, a mixed fleet and a high price of error at dispatch. We assess these things at the audit before work begins, not after.

Support after launch

Rollout does not end with the pilot. After launch 24/7 support is in place: if the accounting system, data format or process changes, we adapt the integration. The model is regularly retrained on new data — this matters because customers, suppliers, assortment and seasonal patterns change over time, and a model without retraining gradually loses accuracy. Once per period we compare actual metrics with targets and propose improvements: new scenarios, connecting other sites or channels, additional reports for managers.

Frequently asked questions

How much does AI reduce fuel consumption through route optimisation?

In practice route optimisation reduces fuel consumption by 25% through more efficient load planning and delivery order.

How does computer vision check order completeness in the warehouse?

Cameras in the warehouse check the assembled order against the specification automatically, which reduces the number of mis-sorts at dispatch.

Which WMS/TMS systems are supported?

The system integrates with the WMS/TMS used in the warehouse without stopping current processes.

What happens if an urgent order is added during the day?

The route is rebuilt accounting for the new order, vehicles already on the road and customer time windows, without recalculating the whole plan again.

Do all vehicles need trackers?

For route optimisation, order, address and vehicle data is enough. GPS trackers are needed for arrival-time forecasting and route execution control.

Can we start only with the warehouse, without transport?

Yes. The modules are independent: you can start with completeness control or warehouse load forecasting and connect routing later.

Does routing account for different types of vehicles and cargo?

Yes. The load capacity and volume of every vehicle, temperature requirements, body types and entry restrictions are passed to the model, so orders are distributed only to suitable transport.
