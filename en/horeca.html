What the solution includes

How it works

Integration with iiko / R-Keeper (timing is indicative, after scope and access are confirmed). The AI learns from your data — menu, sales, bookings. After a week the system predicts demand better than a manager with 10 years of experience.

How each module works

Digital host answers guests in Telegram, the Max messenger and on the website around the clock: takes and moves bookings, confirms the visit the day before, answers questions about the menu, parking and banquets. It sees the guest's history in the CRM — favourite table, allergies, birthday — and hands only non-standard situations to a human host: complaints, large events, disputed cases. Visit reminders are the main tool against no-shows: a guest who has confirmed a booking comes noticeably more often.

Predictive purchasing builds a sales forecast for every menu item 7 days ahead. The model accounts for day of week, season, weather, holidays, bookings and promotions, then converts the forecast into raw materials using the recipe cards. The chef or buyer gets a ready supplier order list and decides whether to send it as is or adjust it. This is where the main saving comes from: fewer products written off at expiry and fewer sold-out bestsellers on Friday evening.

Stock control tracks shelf life and FIFO usage, warns in advance about products that will soon have to be written off, and suggests how to use them — for example in a dish of the day or staff meals.

Menu personalisation suggests to the waiter, or shows in the online order, dishes and drinks a particular guest is likely to enjoy based on their order history. It grows the average check without pushy scripted upsells.

Computer vision in the kitchen — cameras above the pass and workstations check that portions and plating match the standard, and monitor cleanliness and PPE use. Violations are recorded with a photo and time, and the chef sees a per-shift report instead of reviewing recordings by hand.

Rollout stages

  • Audit — we look at what data exists in iiko or R-Keeper, how bookings and purchasing are organised, and where money is being lost today. The result is a list of modules and pilot KPIs.
  • Integration (about 3 days) — we connect to the accounting system, messengers and CRM. The restaurant keeps running: everything is connected in parallel with current processes.
  • Model training — the AI learns from the venue's sales history, menu and bookings. The longer the history in the accounting system, the faster the forecast reaches working accuracy.
  • Pilot at one location — we compare metrics with the fixed KPIs: write-offs, no-shows, average check, guest response time.
  • Rolling out to the chain — after a confirmed result the modules are connected at the other locations and cross-location analytics appears.

What data is needed

To start, what most venues already have is enough: check and menu history in iiko or R-Keeper, recipe cards, the booking log. Perfect order in the data is not required — the model keeps learning during the pilot. If part of the data is kept in spreadsheets, we migrate it during integration.

Typical mistakes when introducing AI in a restaurant

  • Starting with the whole chain at once. If processes differ between locations, one location's problems multiply across all of them. A pilot at one venue is cheaper and shows the effect more honestly.
  • Not involving the chef and the manager. A purchasing forecast works only when it is used. The team must understand where the numbers come from and when they can be corrected by hand.
  • Judging the result by eye. Without KPIs fixed before the start, a month later it is impossible to say whether the project had an effect. That is why we measure write-offs, no-shows and average check before and after.
  • Expecting the bot to replace the host completely. A digital host removes routine, but complex cases and warm contact with regular guests stay with people.

Who it suits

For 1–3 locations the basic set is usually enough — a digital host, integration and CRM with reminders. For chains of 4+ locations predictive purchasing and cross-location analytics matter more, and for chains of 10+ locations — a unified management system, computer vision in kitchens and auto-purchasing from suppliers. Indicative cost — from 650 EUR for 1–3 locations, from 1 650 EUR for 4–10 locations and from 3 850 EUR for a chain of 10+ locations; more in the pricing and the payback calculator.

What it looks like on an ordinary day

In the morning the chef opens the weekly forecast and a ready supplier order: the system has already accounted for Saturday's banquet booking and the rainy weather forecast. During the day the digital host takes bookings in messengers and reminds guests about the evening visit, offering those who have not confirmed to move the time. In the evening the manager receives a summary: how many guests came from bookings, which products are close to expiry, where the kitchen deviated from the standard. The cycle repeats every morning, and the model accounts for what happened yesterday.

What changes for the team

  • The host and hostess stop answering calls and booking messages all evening. Routine questions are closed by the digital host, while people attend to guests in the hall and to complex situations.
  • The chef and the buyer start the day with a ready sales forecast and a supplier order list. Instead of recounting stock from memory, they check and adjust the proposed order.
  • The manager sees write-offs, no-shows, average check and kitchen standard violations in one report and can analyse causes instead of collecting figures from different spreadsheets.
  • The chain owner compares locations against each other on the same metrics and sees where processes work worse and why.

Integrations

We work with the systems already installed in restaurants: iiko and R-Keeper for sales, menu and stock, Remarket for loyalty programmes, Telegram and the Max messenger for guest communication, Mango Office for telephony. Data does not have to be moved by hand — the modules receive it from these systems automatically and return results to the same place.

How we measure the effect

Before the pilot starts we fix baseline metrics for a comparable period: the amount of write-offs, the share of no-shows in bookings, average check and guest response time. After the pilot we compare the same metrics at the same location, adjusted for seasonality. For a quick estimate even before the audit you can use the calculator on the home page — it estimates approximate savings from the number of locations, average check and guest flow.

Frequently asked questions

How much does introducing AI in a restaurant cost?

How does AI reduce write-offs in a restaurant?
