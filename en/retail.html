What the solution includes

  • Auto-purchasing — supplier orders created with no human involved

  • Personalisation — product recommendations based on customer behaviour

How it works

We connect to your accounting system (timing is indicative, after scope and access are confirmed). The AI analyses sales history, stock and deliveries. After 2 weeks the system predicts demand better than Excel with macros.

How each module works

Demand forecast. The model builds a sales forecast for every product and every location — store, warehouse or marketplace. It accounts for seasonality, day of week, weather, holidays, promotions, prices and stock, and for the fact that zero sales on a day with nothing on the shelf do not mean zero demand. The forecast is recalculated automatically as new sales arrive.

Auto-purchasing. Based on the forecast, current stock, lead times and minimum order quantities the system creates supplier orders. The purchasing manager gets a ready document and is responsible for strategic decisions — new suppliers, assortment, terms — not for recalculating thousands of items in a spreadsheet every day.

Marketplace stock management. For sellers on Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex.Market the model suggests how much of which product to ship to which warehouses so as not to lose sales to zero stock and not to overpay for storing dead stock.

Personalised recommendations. In the online shop and mailings the AI selects products based on a particular customer's purchase history: complementary products, a replacement for an unavailable item, timely repeat purchase of consumables.

Assortment analytics. The system shows products that take up space and money but hardly sell, and items whose demand grows faster than stock.

Rollout stages

  • Data audit — we check how sales, stock and purchasing are kept in 1C, MoySklad or marketplace seller accounts.
  • Connection to the accounting system (timeline set once scope and access are confirmed) — export of sales and stock history with no data migration and no interruption of work.
  • Forecast training — the model learns from your sales history for each product and location.
  • Pilot on part of the assortment or one location — we compare shortages, surplus and turnover with the baseline.
  • Launching auto-purchasing — once the forecast is trusted, supplier orders are created automatically.

What data is needed

The main thing is sales and stock history. The longer and more detailed it is (by day, product and location), the more accurate the forecast from the first weeks. Prices, promotion data and supplier lead times also help. The model accounts for weather, holidays and calendar effects itself.

Typical mistakes when introducing AI in retail

  • Training the forecast on “dirty” sales. If a product was not on the shelf, sales were zero not because of demand. We account for out-of-stock periods, otherwise the model “learns” that nobody needs the product.
  • Handing all purchasing to the automatic system at once. First the forecast runs alongside the manager and the team sees where it is more accurate than manual calculation. Auto-purchasing is switched on when it is already trusted.
  • Ignoring supplier constraints. Minimum order quantities, pack multiples and delivery schedules must be in the model, otherwise an order will be mathematically right but impossible to fulfil.
  • Looking only at forecast accuracy. What matters to the business is shortage, surplus, inventory turnover and revenue — those are what we fix as pilot KPIs.

Who it suits

Chain and non-chain retail, distributors, online shops and marketplace sellers — everyone with hundreds or thousands of SKUs whose purchasing is currently calculated by hand or with simple spreadsheet formulas.

What it looks like on an ordinary day

In the morning the buyer opens the supplier order list that the system produced overnight from fresh sales and stock. They see why each product has exactly that quantity proposed: sales forecast, current stock, goods in transit, lead time and minimum order. If a promotion is planned for next week, its parameters are already accounted for. The buyer edits a few items where they know what the model does not — for example an agreement with a supplier on a new price — and sends the orders. Meanwhile the category manager receives a report on products that have not sold for a long time and items that will soon run out in marketplace warehouses. In the evening the day's sales enter the model, and the next morning the forecast is already updated.

Forecasts for new products

For products without their own sales history the model relies on similar items in the same category, prices and seasonality, and switches quickly to the product's own data as real sales appear. This makes it possible to plan purchasing when launching new items instead of waiting for statistics to accumulate.

What changes for the team

  • The purchasing manager instead of recalculating stock in spreadsheets every day, checks ready supplier orders and works on negotiations, assortment and terms.
  • The category manager sees which products “freeze” money and which regularly run out, and makes assortment decisions based on data.
  • The store or warehouse manager runs into empty shelves and surplus that later has to be sold at a discount less often.
  • The business owner gets a forecast of revenue and of working capital needed for purchases over the coming weeks.

Integrations

We connect to 1C and MoySklad for sales, stock and purchasing, and to Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex.Market seller accounts for sales and stock in marketplace warehouses. Ready supplier orders return to the accounting system the team is already used to working in.

How we measure the effect

Before the pilot we fix the share of days with zero stock for key products, the volume of surplus and dead stock, inventory turnover and revenue on the chosen part of the assortment or location. After the pilot we compare the same metrics over a comparable period adjusted for seasonality and promotions.

When rollout does not pay off

If the assortment is small, demand is stable and purchasing takes a manager an hour a week, a forecasting model will not produce noticeable savings — a tidy spreadsheet is enough. The project will also not pay off if sales and stock are not kept in an accounting system and the history cannot be restored: in that case order must first be brought to the accounting, and we will advise where to start. AI gives the greatest effect with a wide assortment, several locations or warehouses and noticeable losses from shortages and surplus.

Support after launch

Rollout does not end with the pilot. After launch 24/7 support is in place: if the accounting system, data format or process changes, we adapt the integration. The model is regularly retrained on new data — this matters because customers, suppliers, assortment and seasonal patterns change over time, and a model without retraining gradually loses accuracy. Once per period we compare actual metrics with targets and propose improvements: new scenarios, connecting other sites or channels, additional reports for managers.

Frequently asked questions

Which systems does AI for retail integrate with?

With 1C, MoySklad, Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex.Market — the timeline for connecting to the accounting system is set once scope and access are confirmed.

How accurate is the demand forecast?

Forecast accuracy is 94%, accounting for seasonality, weather and events, which in practice reduces shortages by 30% and raises revenue by 22%.

Does auto-purchasing completely replace the purchasing manager?

The system creates supplier orders automatically from sales history and stock, but strategic purchasing decisions stay with the manager.

How much sales history is needed for the forecast?

You can start with the history already in your accounting system. The longer and more detailed the history by day, product and location, the faster the forecast reaches working accuracy; the model keeps learning during the pilot.

Is the solution suitable for sellers only on marketplaces?

Yes. We connect to Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex.Market seller accounts and help plan shipments to warehouses so as not to lose sales to zero stock.

How are promotions and seasonality handled?

The model accounts for seasonality, holidays, weather and promotion history. If you plan a promotion, its parameters are passed to the forecast in advance.
